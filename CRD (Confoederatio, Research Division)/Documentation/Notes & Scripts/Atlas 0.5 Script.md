But let's face it, you've seen this all before. So what separates Atlas from other 'Every Year' maps? `[Scene: 'The History of the World: Every Year' videos overlay]`. The answer is that Atlas is a dataset, not a series of static maps. 

History can be queried `[switch between keyframes]`, edited `[jumpcut to brush mode]`, and analysed `[show Stadestér/Eoscala papers]`. Not just by me, but by you as well `[GitHub link]`. It is open-source data, with attributable sourcing `[scroll through sources page]`.

Atlas is also GeoJSON, meaning that it is vector `[clip of Notepad++]`. Maps do not lose resolution as you zoom in. It masks coastlines perfectly, allowing for detailed statistical comparisons. What cities belonged to which country? What was the population of the Roman Empire? Can economic inequality in the Triple Alliance be measured?

And it is much higher resolution than existing options such as Cliopatria `[show slide comparison: 1806]`, or Running Reality `[show slide comparison: 1940]`. And it comes with a map editor - purpose-built for history, both 2000 years ago `[zoom in on the Ancient Near East]`, and 15 minutes ago `[Livemap: Collation]` - which we handcoded to be just as open and moddable as the rest. 

Sure, Atlas may not always be perfectly detailed, but it is a living database, one whose quality can be consistently improved. Static videos or images offer no such advantages. And not only did we build this map editor - we built the code editor used to build it `[click: Script Manager]`. And we built the UI framework that surrounds it. 

No project for the humanities, for history, for demographics, has this level of infrastructure. Because we want to build a digital copy of the world.

This is part of a 5-part series on the Naissance World Model `[animated banner with 5 videos]`, because political borders are only the beginning. Databases on urban growth, population, economics, and modern-day open-source intelligence have already been assembled, complete with open methodologies, and sourcing. Lots of sourcing. 

All that is left is their integration. And that is a task that we at Confoederatio are uniquely equipped for.
